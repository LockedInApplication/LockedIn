# Shared date sheet

## Intent and approaches

LockedIn should make meeting up easy without turning the conversation into a sequence of planning forms. People should be able to find the current place and time without scrolling, notice when they need to respond, and return to exactly where they were chatting.

- **Shared date sheet (chosen):** Open a private plan for this pair from one small chat-header control. A single compact invitation in the transcript gives context; venue discovery, responses and later updates live in the sheet. This costs one tap to see details but gives the conversation its space back.
- **Pinned date card (considered):** Put the place, time and actions directly above the transcript. Faster to glance at, but it permanently takes space when there is a plan.
- **Standalone Upcoming Dates (deferred):** A single destination for dates from multiple conversations. It adds navigation and does not solve the small, in-chat handoff this release is about.

The selected mockup determines where the invitation and actions live. The flow, copy and notification defaults below are proposals for sign-off.

## What people see

- In a writable one-to-one chat, **Plan a date** is a small header control. With an incoming proposal it becomes **Date invite** or **New time proposed**. With a confirmed plan it becomes **View date**. It never adds a permanent place/time card above the transcript. Use a bottom sheet on mobile and the same content in a dialog on desktop; closing returns focus, preserves scroll and leaves the message draft intact.
- With no active plan, the sheet offers the existing venue discovery choices (**Find together** and **Browse on my own**), or **Use a shared place** if there are previously shared venues in this conversation. Reuse the existing shared-search consent, relevance and location behavior. Keep venue details and available photos in the planner. There is no new starting-location prompt unless the existing search actually needs one.
- Choose a result or a shared venue, choose a day and time, and tap **Send invite**. Browsing and picking do not send anything. Keep the selection only while the composer is open; no new persisted draft feature. A venue sent on its own using the existing explicit share action remains an idea, not a scheduled date. No automatic invitations, arbitrary venue text or confirmation inferred from a chat message.
- The initial send leaves **one compact invitation** in the transcript. Do not send a second venue message as part of the invite. Tapping the note opens that date, even if it is no longer the current plan. Where older plans have proposal rows, use the original proposal as their invitation too; keep subsequent updates in that plan's activity. Explicitly shared venue ideas remain separate, ordinary entries. Counters, confirmations, declines, withdrawals and cancellations leave no additional rows in the normal transcript. Nothing is deleted or rewritten.
- The proposal shows the venue, time with zone and who needs to respond. Only the recipient of the current proposal can **Confirm**, choose **Another time**, or decline. A counter replaces the proposed time and waits for the other person's agreement. The proposer can withdraw. Either person can cancel a future confirmed date. A confirmed date can still optionally be exported as a calendar file; no calendar connection or automatic schedule changes. One actionable plan per conversation; resolved or past plans remain read-only and can be opened from their invitation.

## Behavior and data boundaries

- Reuse the existing planner, single active plan, revision and expiry rules, chat permissions, transaction boundaries, server-authored evidence, and post-commit invalidation. Keep normal rate limits, closure and blocking enforcement. Apply in both accepted Discovery chats and direct-invite/shared-space chats. Losing chat write access still bars all plan mutations, including cancellation; date viewing follows existing chat access rules. This work does not change mutual lock-in or Discovery visibility.
- The API resolves a venue either from an authorized shared-place message or from an existing server-signed suggestion ticket scoped to the actor and conversation. A new result may be proposed directly, without first creating a standalone venue-share row. Store the trusted venue anchor and first invitation atomically with the plan and preserve exact-retry behavior even if the ticket has since expired. Do not make a live provider lookup a prerequisite for sending a still-valid pick or responding to a plan. If suggestions or details are unavailable, already shared venues and saved plans still work.
- Keep all plan transitions as immutable server-authored records for moderation, history, and retries. Store only the agreed public place and time along with required plan metadata; do not put private areas, coordinates tied to either person, or raw provider capabilities in chat or alerts. Individual updates remain reportable from plan activity; the only evidence included is what was shared and visible for that update.
- Give the other participant a quiet in-app **date update** in the existing notification surface and an unread indicator on the chat-header action if their in-app activity preference allows it. Opting out hides both kinds of alert but does not hide the current plan or its response controls. Submitting a change does not alert its sender. Alerts contain no venue or time. A canceled or declined plan stays reachable from its old invite and, if alerts are on, from its unread update even though it has left the active slot.
- In the sheet experience, the invite and subsequent updates count as date activity only, even when the invite leaves a chat breadcrumb. Keep an independent plan receipt or equivalent so opening a sheet cannot clear an unread text and scrolling the chat cannot silently clear a counteroffer. Acknowledge only visible, authorized details; viewing an old plan via its breadcrumb must not clear newer updates on another plan. Preserve genuinely unread legacy activity at cutover without resurrecting events people already read. Keep normal chat ordering and pagination predictable; hiding or collapsing date updates must not create apparent history gaps or shift the viewport.
- **Mixed versions:** Clients that already support the current date planner must still see and respond to the same plan using their existing inline UI. Only sheet-capable clients receive compact presentation and independent acknowledgements; keep the existing transcript and acknowledgement contract for legacy clients, and do not require both people to update before they can plan together. Older clients without planner actions retain safe plain-text fallback. Keep one plan and one underlying set of records for everyone, not a second plan for the new UI.
- If planning is off, do not expose scheduling or date-detail capabilities. Explicitly shared venue ideas and plain chat remain usable as before. No deployment, production flag change or external calendar/phone alerts are part of this design review.

## What we will verify before release

- Two accounts in each chat type: discover and review a venue, send exactly one invite without a preliminary chat post, counter and confirm, then cancel. Prove the counter and cancellation are discoverable even while a sheet is closed and that past invitations reopen the correct read-only date.
- Existing shared-place proposals, search disabled/down, expired or foreign tickets, concurrent sends, stale revisions, blocked or closed chats, and a deployment with planning turned off.
- Quiet refresh, independent unread state, alert opt-out, a conversation with different client versions, unchanged text drafts and anchors (including paginated chat), and mobile/desktop interaction with keyboard dismissal and focus return. Compare the chat before and after multiple plan updates.

The builder chooses components, endpoint shape, message projection and receipt schema, provided they meet these observable behaviors. Calendar sharing to friends, multi-slot polls, bookings, automated scheduling, and private post-date check-ins are out of scope.
